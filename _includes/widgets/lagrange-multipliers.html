Both maps show actual height fields over −1.25 ≤ x,y ≤ 1.25. They share the same camera and scales, with vertical height compressed equally for readability. Surfaces are translucent so contours remain visible. The stronger horizontal contour passes through P; the green path follows g = 1, rising and falling on the objective graph. Drag either surface to rotate both, or use the camera sliders in Settings.

A gradient is a vector in the input plane, not the normal to the 3D graph. Dashed coloured arrows show normalized gradient directions in that plane. Solid coloured arrows lift these directions to the graph’s tangent plane; black arrows lift the chosen unit direction. Their heights encode directional derivatives. A lifted arrow is a tangent vector, not a path remaining on the surface.

Position angle moves P along g = 1. In Contours from above, dragging also moves P. Direction from tangent spans a full turn: 0° and ±180° are tangent, +90° is outward normal, and −90° inward. Follow ∇f and Follow ∇g choose the respective steepest ascent directions. The maximum slope is the gradient’s magnitude.

The curve profile uses the ray angle θ on its horizontal axis; its reported slope is along the unit tangent (per unit arc length). Minimum point and Maximum point are numerical solutions of the multiplier equations. Reset restores the starting point and tangent direction while preserving the map, profile, and camera. All changes are undoable. This short experiment does not save progress.
