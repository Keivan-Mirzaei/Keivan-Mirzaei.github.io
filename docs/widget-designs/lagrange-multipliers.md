# Lagrange multipliers

- **Stable IDs:** Registry `lagrange-multipliers`; descriptive article anchor `lagrange-sections`.
- **Purpose:** Explore noncircular contours and gradient directions on two linked height fields; verify steepest ascent by changing a unit direction; connect contour normals to a constrained minimum.
- **Model:** `f=(x−2)²+(x−2)(y−1)+3(y−1)²`; `g=x²+2y²+(x−y)⁴/4`; constraint `g=1`. The objective has tilted elliptical contours; the quartic constraint has level-dependent oval contours. Gradients and Hessians are analytic. The feasible point is the positive ray intersection, obtained by solving a quadratic in squared radius.
- **3D maps:** Orthographic projections of sampled height fields over `−1.25≤x,y≤1.25`, with exact analytic contours. Both maps use the same camera and coordinate scales; vertical height is compressed by 0.08 equally. Translucent surfaces retain visibility of contour lines. The selected horizontal level is highlighted; the feasible curve is lifted onto each graph. The 3D maps render with native SVG and have no external renderer or perpetual animation.
- **Arrows:** Dashed coloured arrows show normalized gradient directions in the input plane. Solid coloured arrows lift those unit directions into the graph's tangent plane, with vertical component equal to the directional derivative. Black arrows lift the selected unit direction. The gradient and the graph normal are explicitly distinguished. Only the top-down diagram uses raw gradient vectors at a common scale of 0.12.
- **Controls:** A shared Map dropdown selects 3D surfaces or contours from above. Position angle θ follows the feasible curve. Dragging a surface rotates both maps; dragging the top-down diagram moves the point by ray projection. The full-turn direction slider runs from −180° to 180° relative to the constraint's unit tangent. Follow ∇f and Follow ∇g are actions selecting the corresponding maximizing direction, with actual slope and maximum slope displayed together.
- **Profiles:** A shared dropdown chooses straight-line cross sections `h(P+tv)` or the objective along the exact constraint. Straight sections use a common `−0.65≤t≤0.65` horizontal interval, separate labelled vertical axes, short tangent segments, and the `g=1` reference. The curved profile uses ray angle in degrees; its accompanying derivative is explicitly per unit arc length, not per degree.
- **Extrema:** Minimum and maximum presets use bisection of the analytic tangent slope in 35°–36° and 221°–222°. They satisfy the multiplier equations to numerical precision. Strictly convex/concave Lagrangians certify their global roles. Numerical presets are recognized within native-control serialization precision; nearby nonstationary slopes remain nonzero. The minimum has the shared green completion effect; the maximum removes it. Gradient-ascent success is confirmed independently in text.
- **Settings and Info:** Shared native disclosures start closed, stay open during outside interaction, and are mutually exclusive. Escape closes the active panel and returns focus. Settings contains numeric camera rotation/elevation sliders and an undoable Reset view action, providing keyboard alternatives to dragging.
- **History:** One drag or slider gesture is one action. Undo/Redo covers points, directions, named choices, camera changes, and actions. Reset restores the starting point and tangent direction while preserving map/profile/camera preferences. Cancelled drags restore the prior state. Listeners and observers are disposed on navigation. Instances have independent history and SVG definition IDs.
- **Precision and fallback:** Interface labels and outputs use the shared font and three significant digits; structured fixed expressions use semantic MathML. Six decimal places in the worked solution make numerical root reproduction possible. Known zero tangent slopes are represented as zero; small nonzero results retain scientific notation. Five self-contained SVG previews plus the identifying mark come from the same model via `scripts/lagrange_figures.mjs`. No saving or unrelated controls are needed.
- **Checks:** Feasibility, tangent/normal orthogonality, the full directional-slope bound and equality cases, independent finite-difference gradients, noncircular analytic contours, multiplier residuals, global Hessian certificates, lifted arrows, common projection scales, entropy, UI history, shared camera, disclosures, and phone layout.
- **Exceptions:** None. Ascent and stationary-point buttons perform specific mathematical actions; named modes remain dropdowns and numerical parameters remain sliders.
